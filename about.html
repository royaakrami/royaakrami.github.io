Electrical Engineering · Colorado School of Mines · Class of 2027

Let's make hardware and software work with each other.

I'm an electrical engineering student at Colorado School of Mines. My research has taken me through machine learning, signal processing, biomechanics, spectroscopy, robotics, embedded systems, heliophysics, exoplanet science, and so much more.

What I'm working on right now

I'm currently a Research Assistant at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. I'm also the Electrical Subsystem Lead for Colorado School of Mines' NASA Lunabotics team. This means I spend much of my time training my 1D convolutional neural network to detect anomalies in noisy exoplanet transit light curves from JPL. I also develop circuit diagrams and PCB designs for our Lunabotics rover.

What I've worked on

At Phal Lab, I worked on quantum cascade laser-based spectroscopy and coauthored a paper on our work. I also independently implemented a 2D LiDAR mapping system.

Through projects in embedded systems, digital logic, and PCB design, I've gained experience connecting hardware and software. In Systems Exploration and Engineering Design (SEED) Lab, I'm putting these skills into practice with a Raspberry Pi- and Arduino-based robot, working on camera calibration, marker detection, and motion control.

My research has also taken me to NASA Goddard, where I used a residual neural network to model hydrogen Lyman-alpha emission for exospheric mapping, and ETH Zürich, where I developed a MATLAB pipeline to estimate foot placement and assess gait stability using wearable IMUs.

What it means to me

I love electrical engineering because it lets me connect physical systems with the code and data that bring them to life. There’s something satisfying about seeing a circuit work or a program run, but what excites me most is making them work together: turning individual components into something that can sense, respond, and move.

Outside of my career

I've fostered 10 cats, and I'm getting a new foster soon! I also play around on Garageband every now and then. If we ever meet, ask to see what's in my backpack. There will 100% be a MIDI keyboard in there.
