Electrical Engineering · Colorado School of Mines · Class of 2027

I like when a physical system and its data start making sense together.

I'm an electrical engineering student at Colorado School of Mines. My research has taken me through machine learning, signal processing, biomechanics, spectroscopy, robotics, embedded systems, heliophysics, and exoplanet science.

What I'm working on now

I'm currently a Research Assistant at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. I'm also the Electrical Subsystem Lead for Colorado School of Mines' NASA Lunabotics team. This means most of my time is spent training my 1D convolutional neural network to detect anomalous exoplanet transit signals in noisy light-curve data from JPL, while also developing wiring diagrams and PCB designs for our Lunabotics rover.

Research across different fields

This summer, from May through August 2026, I did movement-biomechanics research at ETH Zürich. There I built a MATLAB pipeline that uses wearable IMU data to estimate foot placement and evaluate gait stability.

Before that, at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, I processed H-Lyman-alpha intensity data in JupyterHub and MATLAB and used a 28-layer residual neural network to model emission patterns for exospheric mapping.

What connects it all

I'm most interested in research where engineering tools make complex scientific data easier to understand, validate, and use. I'm happiest when a real physical system meets data-driven modeling.
